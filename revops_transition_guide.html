RevOps · Career Development

Career transition guide

For the major inflection points in the RevOps career path. These transitions aren't just promotions — they are identity shifts. Use this guide to have the honest conversations that those moments require.

1Before the conversation
Both people complete their reflection fields independently. Don't share answers yet. The goal is two honest starting points, not one person anchoring the other.
2During the conversation
Use the critical questions as prompts, not a script. Let the conversation go where it needs to. The synthesis button helps capture what you agreed on afterward.
3After the conversation
Record the outcome decision and next steps. This document should be a living record — come back to it at the next review cycle to track how things have developed.
L5 / L6 Lead → M1 Team Lead
IC to management
From owning your work to owning other people's outcomes
The identity shift
An IC's identity is built around what they personally produce — the quality of their code, their analysis, their solutions. A manager's identity has to be built around what their team produces. On day one of management, the thing you were best at — your craft — is no longer the primary job. Some people discover they love this. Others discover they don't. This conversation exists to find out which one is true before the transition happens, not after.
M2 Manager → M3 Sr Manager
Manager to functional owner
From running a team to owning a domain end-to-end
The identity shift
A Manager's job is reliable team delivery. A Senior Manager's job is domain strategy — deciding where the domain should go, not just executing where it's been pointed. This means being accountable for outcomes you can't directly control, developing managers instead of just ICs, and being comfortable saying "I don't know how we'll get there yet, but here's where we're going." The hardest part isn't the additional scope — it's tolerating the ambiguity that comes with it.
M3 / M4 Director → VP / SVP
Functional owner to executive
From owning a domain to building the function itself
The identity shift
A Director owns a domain. A VP owns the whole function — which means no domain is fully theirs anymore. Every decision is now a tradeoff between competing priorities, every conversation is about the health of the whole, and the primary craft is building the organization itself: hiring the right leaders, setting the culture, deciding what RevOps stands for. The people who struggle with this transition are usually the ones who can't let go of being the expert. The people who thrive are the ones who find genuine satisfaction in making everyone else successful.
